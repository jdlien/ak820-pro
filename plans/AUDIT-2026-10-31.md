# Firmware audit, 2026-10-31

A one-off audit of the whole firmware, run from Buzz by a multi-agent
workflow: 12 area reviewers, 3 cross-cutting lenses (interrupts, time and
integer arithmetic, power loss), 8 libFuzzer targets on the real source
files (about 4 hours and 669 M executions), then a verifier per finding that
tried to refute it and then wrote a host test against the unedited source,
with a skeptic on the two highest. Nothing was flashed and no device was
opened; white's drain was not touched.

**The tests are not in the repo.** They live on Buzz in
`~/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/` (`verify-NNN/` per finding, `fuzz-*/` per
target, with harnesses, stubs and reproducers; the corpora were not kept).
When a finding is fixed, its test moves into `scripts/` with the fix, as a
regression test. Paths below that start with that folder are Buzz-only.

Checked by hand after the run (Buzz's session): F-001 (the per-byte
`spiExchange()` under a 1024-byte slice, `lcd_bus.c:733`, and QMK's
`raw_hid_task()` draining every queued report) and F-002 (`"command": true`,
QMK's default `IS_COMMAND()` of both Shifts, B and Esc to `reset_keyboard()`)
both hold; F-001's real-world cost is during asset provisioning only, so
high rather than critical. F-002's and F-009's tests rerun from the folder
above. Not yet checked by hand: the rest.

Also from 10-31, outside this report: two unanchored reviews of the 10-28
deep-sleep reset (`review-codex-ds-wake-unanchored-2026-10-31.md`,
`review-fable-ds-wake-unanchored-2026-10-31.md`). Codex's unbounded CT16B3
spin in the wake's restore is not in this report: the sleep reviewer

## Summary

What ran:

- **12 area reviewers**: radio link, lock-LED channel and text, raw HID, sleep/power/watchdog, display, LCD bus and SPI, clock, battery and LEDs, menu core, menu profiles, core and init, local core patches. Each read its files in full and followed calls outward as far as needed.
- **3 cross-cutting lenses** across the board directory: concurrency, time arithmetic, power loss.
- **8 libFuzzer targets** on the modules that build on a host: the lock-LED decoder, the raw81 collector, the text and clock helpers, settings and profiles, the menu, the CH582F receive path, raw-HID dispatch, the battery gauge. 14,215 s of fuzzing (about 4 hours) and about 669 million executions.
- **A verifier per finding**, told to refute it and to write a host test against the unedited source. The two highest-rated findings also went to a skeptic.

The numbers: 58 raw reports, 49 after merging the ones found by more than one reviewer or fuzzer. Of the 49, **43 are confirmed, 1 is plausible, 4 need the bench and 1 was refuted**. By corrected severity the 44 confirmed or plausible findings are 1 critical, 1 high, 16 medium and 26 low. There is no memory-safety bug in the board's own code. The two out-of-bounds reads are both in upstream code (F-033, F-034).

The ones that matter most:

1. **F-002, high.** QMK Command is compiled into every build, v0.2.0's daily included. Holding both Shifts and pressing B or Esc enters the bootloader; Backspace formats the whole emulated EEPROM, profiles included; 1 or 3 makes the Fn layer the base layer. Rolling from one Shift to the other while typing capitals is enough: "NB" typed with opposite Shifts jumps to the bootloader. The fix is one line in `keyboard.json`.
2. **F-001, critical by the rubric.** Every flash-channel CRC slice is 1024 per-byte ChibiOS SPI round trips, roughly 40-200 ms. So the shipped `ak820ctl` stalls the main loop past 25 ms on every verify slice, hundreds of times per provisioning. A host that sends CRC commands without reading the replies keeps `raw_hid_task` looping until the watchdog resets the board. The critical rating rests on that hostile-host case; the everyday harm is the provisioning stalls.
3. **F-003, medium.** An abandoned pairing (hold a slot key, pair nothing, walk away) keeps the board awake on battery indefinitely: about 27 mA instead of about 3 mA, with nothing on the dark screen to say so.
4. **F-004, medium.** On the Mac side, a knob detent drops a Shift or Option the user is holding, and a Shift tapped mid-spin turns the rest of the spin into Alt+Volume, which opens macOS Sound settings. Every build.
5. **F-009, medium.** This explains the open BACKLOG item "Whole-second clock slips". `RTC_GET_TIME` takes the seconds and the fraction from two different samples. The host model reads 1 GET in about 1,700 exactly 1 s low; the logs show 4 slips in about 5,170 syncs. The fix is a few lines in `hid_protocol.c`.
6. **F-005 and F-006, medium.** Two ways the clock goes wrong on battery with no host. A reset in a PCF write's STOP window leaves the PCF frozen, and the face then replays one minute forever. A failed first PCF read leaves 00:00 until a host sync; the retry `rtc.h` promises never existed.
7. **F-045, needs the bench.** Every wake turns the USB PHY on without ESD_EN, whatever the USB driver left. It is a candidate for lead (b) of the 10-28 deep-sleep reset. Its verifier also found that the USB LLD can undo its own suspend on a stale SOF flag.

One housekeeping item: a fuzzing agent ran the repo's `build.sh` by mistake and left three files in `ak820pro-builds/out/`. See "Things the audit itself touched" near the end.

## How to read this

- file:line references are relative to `qmk_firmware-ak820pro/`. The headers give the full path. In the prose, board files are named by their path inside `keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/`, for example `bluetooth/ch582f_ajazz.c:1636`. The SN32 low-level drivers named by file name alone (`hal_usb_lld.c`, `hal_rtc_lld.c`, `hal_spi_v2_lld.c`, `hal_serial_lld.c`) are under `lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/ports/SN32/LLD/SN32F2xx/` in their `USB/`, `RTC/`, `SPI/` and `UART/` directories; `ak820ctl.c` is in `time-util-ak820pro/` at the repo root, outside the firmware tree.
- "Severity" is the verifier's corrected severity. The reported one is shown when it changed.
- Each finding's test lives in its own `verify-NNN` directory under `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/`. The table names only that directory; each section gives the full path and the command.
- "audit #n" is the entry's `n` in `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/findings.json`, which keeps the verified list unchanged and in its original order. The F-number to `n` map is at the end.
- The severity rubric the audit used: critical means a watchdog reset, wedge or brick reachable by any host process or by normal use; high means lost keystrokes or stalls of 25 ms or more in normal use; medium means real misbehaviour on an unusual but possible sequence (power loss mid-write, a malformed host report, a cable pull at a bad moment); low means cosmetic, dev-only or very unlikely.

## Confirmed and plausible findings

### F-001 · critical · Flash-channel CRC slices stall the main loop; a streaming host wedges raw HID until the watchdog fires

`keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/hid_protocol.c:199` · confirmed · severity unchanged · audit #5 · found by raw-hid, lcd-bus-spi, fuzz-rawhid

**What is wrong.** `FC_CRC32` and `FC_CRC_NEXT` fold `CRC_SLICE` = 1024 bytes inside one raw-HID callback (`hid_protocol.c:194-199`, `:286-287`). The comment sizes that at "~0.3 ms of SPI", which was true of the bare-metal SPI of Stage D (`ee337b5913`). Since `6554554d82` (08-27) SPI1 runs under the ChibiOS driver, and `flash_crc32_acc` does one `spiExchange()` per byte (`graphics/lcd_bus.c:733-734`, through `spi1_rw` at `:565-567`). Each byte masks interrupts, suspends the thread, waits for the SPI1 IRQ at priority 3 behind the row ISR (which takes about 73% of the CPU), and switches back. The code's own note at `graphics/lcd_bus.c:295-297` puts a single-byte driver call at about 0.2 ms of wall time at this ISR load. One slice is therefore somewhere around 40-200 ms; its floor is over 1 ms.

The worse effect comes from QMK. `raw_hid_task()` drains every queued OUT report in one call (`tmk_core/protocol/chibios/usb_main.c:517-521`). The queue holds 4 reports and re-arms as each is consumed, at a 1 ms poll. `flash_command` has no per-pass budget, unlike `rtc_pass_used_bus()` (`rtc/rtc.c:1266`). If a host keeps CRC commands queued without reading the replies, `raw_hid_task` never returns: `keyboard_task` never runs, and `watchdog_kick()` (`ak820pro.c:668`, the only kick) is never reached. The watchdog resets the board after about 12 s with the site `WDT_SITE_RAW_HID`, and the RAM battery log and the RTC trim go with it. Three resets inside 10 minutes put the watchdog in degraded mode, and the board stays dead for as long as the host keeps sending.

**Trigger.** (a) The shipped tool: `ak820ctl flash write` or `flash crc`. It waits for each reply, so it does not livelock, but every slice is one main-loop pass of tens of milliseconds or more: about 200 back to back for the asset blob, 544 for the 557 KB font-test upload of 10-28. Keys typed meanwhile can be lost. (b) Any process that sends `FC_CRC32` with length 0xFFFFFF, then `FC_CRC_NEXT` (`[07 11 09]`) in a loop without reading, for more than 12 s.

**Test:** none run.
The verifier was cut off while writing a harness (`verify-005/` holds only its start). The verdict rests on the code path, which it quoted step by step, including the shipped dev ELF's `spiExchange` disassembly: `cpsid i`, `spi_lld_exchange`, then always `chThdSuspendTimeoutS`.

**Corrections.** Trigger (a) does not livelock; it gives repeated stalls of 25 ms or more, which is high by the rubric. The page flush in `FC_WRITE_DATA` is about 12-50 ms depending on whose per-byte figure you take.

**Skeptic.** Could not refute it, and thinks the stall is longer than estimated: about 200 ms per slice, which fits the 1.47 MB write of 10-28 taking about 10 minutes. The skeptic's counterargument on severity: the watchdog path needs a host program that deliberately floods the endpoint; nothing in the repo does that, and a process with raw-HID access can already erase the asset flash or reset the EEPROM through VIA. On that view this is high (or medium, given how rarely anyone provisions), not critical.

**Fix.** Give the flash channel a per-pass budget: a static `flash_pass_used` set after one CRC slice or page program and cleared at the end of housekeeping; while it is set, the flash commands answer `FS_BUSY` without consuming anything. `ak820ctl`'s `fcmd()` already retries `FS_BUSY` up to 2,000 times. Bound the slice by elapsed time (2-3 ms on `st_lld_get_counter()`) or by 64-128 bytes; the host already loops on `FS_MORE`. Fix the stale comment. Optionally cap `raw_hid_task` at a few reports per call, a small core patch that bounds every raw-HID command.

**Bench (optional).** On a spare board with the battery log dumped: read `HC_GET2` and the `HC_ACCT` slow-pass ring, run `ak820ctl flash crc 0xCE0000 1024` once, read them again. The newest ring entry is the slice time. Repeat with 64 bytes for the per-byte cost.

### F-002 · high · QMK Command is on in every build: both Shifts + B/Esc/Backspace/1 enter the bootloader, wipe the EEPROM or swap the base layer

`keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/keyboard.json:22` · confirmed · severity unchanged · audit #27 · found by core-init

**What is wrong.** `keyboard.json:22` has `"command": true`. The build's cflags carry `-DCOMMAND_ENABLE`, and `quantum/command.o` is in daily and fpb, so releases ship it; the v0.2.0 daily ELF contains `command_proc`, `command_common` and `command_extra`. Nothing redefines `IS_COMMAND()`, so it is `get_mods() == MOD_MASK_SHIFT` (`quantum/command.h:37`), and `register_code()` hands every basic keycode to `command_proc()` while both Shifts are held (`quantum/action.c:957-959`). The board's `command_extra()` (`ak820pro.c:739-742`) rolls back an open RGB edit and returns false, so everything goes ahead:

- **B or Esc**: `reset_keyboard()`, the ROM bootloader. The board looks dead, and leaving it takes a cold power-off (`docs/hardware.md:55`).
- **Backspace**: `eeconfig_init()` formats the whole 1 KB emulated EEPROM: the VIA keymap and macros, the kb block, all seven profiles. Nothing backs it up on this path. It also forces the default layer to WINBASE whatever the Win/Mac switch says, and sets the connection host to USB, so on BT or 2.4G every later keystroke goes to USB until the slider or a slot key moves it back.
- **1 or 3 (or F1, F3)**: the Fn layer becomes the base layer. Esc on WINFN and MACFN is `QK_BOOT`, so the next plain Esc enters the bootloader; Q/W/E switch BT slots; digits send F-keys.
- **C**: the CONSOLE state, which has no timeout and swallows h and / until q or Esc.
- Every other Command key (V D X K M S H E N / and the rest) is swallowed while both Shifts are held.

**Trigger.** Holding both Shifts happens when rolling from one to the other while typing capitals or symbols. "NB" (N with Left Shift, B with Right Shift) enters the bootloader; "?!" makes WINFN the base layer; "TH", "AM", "SK" drop the second letter. Works on the release build, on any transport, with no host involved. Shifts + Backspace or Esc is harder to hit by accident, because the same pinky would have to hold the Shift.

**Test:** `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-027/cmd_test.c`

```sh
/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-027/run.sh
```

Result: compiles the real `quantum/command.c`, `eeconfig.c`, `nvm_eeconfig.c`, `connection.c`, the EEPROM driver and the board-patched wear leveling, plus `command_extra` and `layer_switch_get_layer` extracted verbatim. All checks passed, meaning the bug is shown: B and Esc each call `reset_keyboard` once; Backspace erases the store (profile magic byte 0x00, was 0xA4; VIA keymap byte 0x00, was 0x5A), moves the host from BLUETOOTH to USB and sets the default layer to WINBASE; both Shifts + 1 make WINFN the base with Esc resolving to `QK_BOOT`; 10 of 10 other Command keys are swallowed.

**Corrections.** The finding said 4-9 and F4-F9 make the keyboard dead. They do not: with 4 dynamic layers, `MAX_LAYER` is 4 and the layer scan falls back to layer 0, so those keys give WINBASE (on the Mac side, Windows modifiers). Layers 8 and 9 truncate to 0 in an 8-bit layer state.

**Skeptic.** Could not refute it. Counterargument: Command is a standard QMK feature inherited from fpb's port, and the project knows it is on. `docs/hardware.md:887` recommends both Shifts + S, N or H; `docs/display.md:514` notes the NKRO toggle is "easy to hit by accident"; the P1 design let the magic EEPROM reset proceed on purpose. But no document weighs or accepts the bootloader jump, the wipe or the layer swap.

**Fix.** Set `"command": false` in `keyboard.json`. `command_extra` disappears with it (it sits inside `#ifdef COMMAND_ENABLE`). If an NKRO toggle is still wanted, bind `NK_TOGG` in VIA. Update `docs/display.md:514` and `docs/hardware.md:887`. A check in `build.sh` that the daily ELF has no `command_proc` symbol would keep it off.

### F-003 · medium · An abandoned pairing blocks deep sleep until a slot key is pressed

`keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/bluetooth/ch582f_ajazz.c:1636` · confirmed · severity unchanged · audit #0 · found by radio-link

**What is wrong.** `is_pairing` is set in `ch582_enter_pairing()` before the module confirms (`bluetooth/ch582f_ajazz.c:1636`) and again on 5B 31 (`:2121`). Only 5B 32, 33, 34, 36 and the bounce's completion clear it; there is no timeout. The 5B 23 that ends an unanswered advertising window falls into the switch's default case (`:2167-2184`), which leaves the state alone, and the `PA_PAIR` give-up after 12 tries (`:1868-1870`) leaves it too. `sleep.c:269` turns `ch582_is_pairing()` into `DSB_PAIRING`, which has no expiry, and `sleep.c:960-963` refuses every deep sleep while any blocker is set. The repo records the unobserved end of advertising only as a display gap (`plans/CONFIG-MENU-P1-DESIGN.md:1250`), with the menu's idle close as the backstop; nobody noticed it also holds the board awake.

**Trigger.** On battery in BT, hold Fn+Q/W/E for 2 s (or start a pair from the menu), pair nothing, walk away. The board stays awake at about 27 mA instead of about 3.2 mA asleep, roughly an 8x faster idle drain (about 6 days on the 4,000 mAh pack instead of weeks), until someone taps a slot key or uses the menu's Stop.

**What is wrong.** `usb_phy_onI()` sets `USB_CFG.PHY_EN` at every deep-sleep exit whenever it reads clear (`sleep.c:70-74`, called at `:608`, result discarded at `:625`). It never sets `ESD_EN` and never asks why the bit is clear. The SN32 USB LLD clears both bits together when the bus suspends (`lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/ports/SN32/LLD/SN32F2xx/USB/hal_usb_lld.c:240-244`), and every LLD path writes them together. So after a real cable pull, a wake leaves PHY on and ESD off on a floating bus, a state the awake board never has. It is a candidate for lead (b) of the 10-28 site-27 reset on gremlin, an interrupt storm at the unlock; `plans/BACKLOG.md` (about line 169) already lists `usb_phy_onI()` as that lead. What the finding adds is the ESD_EN pairing.

**Test:** `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-009/harness.c`

```sh
/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-009/build.sh && /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-009/t_phy_wake   (fix sketch: build_fixed.sh, then fixed/t_phy_wake_fixed)
```

Result: the real `sleep.c`, `hal_usb_lld.c` and `hal_usb.c` stepped through a sleep. After a suspend alone: PHY on and ESD off at the unlock with the driver SUSPENDED. After a suspend with the SOF status also latched: the same registers, but the driver back to ACTIVE. With no suspend: both on. One USB interrupt with an empty INSTS afterwards turns both on. The fix variant leaves the registers as the LLD left them in every case.

**What the verifier corrected.** The finding said the driver holds SUSPENDED after a pull and that the bench never sees this state. But the same ISR pass undoes a suspend when a stale SOF status bit is latched (`hal_usb_lld.c:302-315`), and QMK enables no SOF interrupt, so the bit can stay latched. That leaves ACTIVE with both bits off, which matches what the project measured after frames stop (`sleep.c:43-46`). If so, the uhubctl bench cut has been exercising PHY-on/ESD-off all along, and the novelty argument falls. The storm itself is unshown. Corrected severity: low; critical if the bench ties it to the 10-28 reset.

**Seen on hardware (10-31).** Black's census at the unmask of a cable-out wake (`gremlin/ds-phase` `05ef0f5bbc9`, op 24 slot 11) read `INSTS` = `BUS_SUSPEND`, `INTEN` with `BUS_IE`, and `CFG` with `PHY_EN` on and `ESD_EN` off: this state, on the board. It raised no USB interrupt in the ~1 ms window (`plans/GREMLIN-RESULTS.md` §21).

**Bench.** Dev build: snapshot `SN_USB->CFG`, `INSTS` and the driver state at the lock and just after the unlock into a no-init slot; log whether `BUS_SUSPEND` carried `USB_SOF`; count USB interrupt entries in the 500 ms after the unlock. Compare a relay-2 + uhubctl cut, the cable fully out, and 10-28's entry (idle past the ladder on USB, pull, asleep about 2 s later, untouched at least 10 minutes).

**Fix.** Save `PHY_EN | ESD_EN` under the lock before the teardown and restore exactly that after the wake (also in the dev `light_task`). Separately, look at the stale-SOF undo in `hal_usb_lld.c`: if the driver can never stay suspended, `usb_wakeup_try()` never fires either.

### F-046 · low · Three 5C zeros in one burst cut the RGB and show 0% even at the top clamp

`keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/battery.c:1289` · needs-hardware · reported medium · audit #48 · found by fuzz-battery

**What is wrong.** `protection_tick()` cuts the lights and sets the level to 0 as soon as three zero reports arrive in a row (`battery.c:1287-1299`), before the top-clamp check and whatever the estimate says. The run counts reports, not polls (`:372`), and the module answers each 5 s poll with three reports in one burst; whether those three are one measurement repeated is unchecked (`docs/battery.md`). The estimator's trimmed mean would discard them. The cut is latched, lifts only after 5 s of USB, and holds the level at 0 meanwhile.

**Test:** `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-048/zero_burst_test.c`

```sh
/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-048/build.sh && /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-048/test_fw trigger; /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-048/test_patched trigger
```

Result: the real `battery.c` and `power.c` through `scripts/battery_sim`. One `0,0,0` burst at the top clamp (estimate 100.00, 4036 mV): one tick later the level is 0%, the RGB cut, "Low: RGB off"; five minutes of 100s later still cut; USB lifts it after 7 s. A burst that does not end in three zeros does not cut. With `&& !c5_top(m)` the trigger no longer cuts, sustained zeros at the top still cut after 10 s, and the simulator's own protection scenarios still pass.

**Why it needs the bench.** The rule is deliberate (comment at `battery.c:1244`, codex's 09-28 plan review, the simulator's `burst()` test), chosen before the three-report bursts were known (10-07). **Fix.** Skip the zero rule at the top clamp, or require the zeros to span two polls (cut when the run is 3+ and 2.5 s or more old). Write down in `docs/battery.md` whether a burst counts as independent evidence.

### F-047 · low · The software I2C has no bus clear and drives SDA push-pull high

`lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/lib/fallback/I2C/hal_i2c_lld.c:40` · needs-hardware · severity unchanged · audit #22 · found by clock

**What is wrong.** With `SW_I2C_USE_OPENDRAIN FALSE`, the error path and every 1-bit drive SDA push-pull high (`lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/lib/fallback/I2C/hal_i2c_lld.c:40-46`). Every transaction starts with an arbitration read that returns before any SCL clock if SDA is low (`:164-166`); `i2c_lld_start()` only sets both lines high; `pcf_xfer()`'s restart sends no clocks (`rtc/rtc.c:308-314`). If the master stops mid-read while the PCF drives a 0 (a reset mid-transaction), the PCF8563, which has no bus timeout and runs off the pack, keeps SDA low, and no code path ever pulls SCL low again unless the MCU reads its own driven high. The missing bus clear is partly documented as accepted (`history/hardening/findings-bounded-wait.md` #7) for the clock-stretch case, on the assumption that the slave releases on its own; a PCF8563 never does.

**Test:** `/Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-022/i2c_wedge_test.c`

```sh
cd /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-022 && LLD=/Users/jdlien/code/ak820-pro/qmk_firmware-ak820pro/lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/lib/fallback/I2C && /opt/homebrew/opt/llvm/bin/clang -std=gnu11 -g -O1 -Wall -Wextra -Wno-unused-parameter -fsanitize=address,undefined -fno-sanitize-recover=undefined -Istubs -I$LLD i2c_wedge_test.c $LLD/hal_i2c_lld.c -o i2c_wedge_test && ./i2c_wedge_test
```

Result: the real `hal_i2c_lld.c` against a bus model with a PCF-like slave. If the PCF wins the contention: 20 retries, never OK, zero SCL falls, the pins fighting the whole time. If the MCU wins: the first retry succeeds. A bus clear (SDA released, up to 9 SCL pulses, START and STOP with SCL high) frees it even when the PCF wins; a first attempt that let SCL fall once more before the STOP failed.

**Why it needs the bench.** Which side wins is analog. The MCU sources at least 12 mA high; the PCF sinks at least 3 mA; a rough divider puts the pad around 2.0-2.8 V, above or near VIH, so the MCU most likely wins and the bus recovers on the next transaction. The trigger is also close to unreachable (about 1e-5 per slider flip). If the PCF wins, though, it is a permanent wedge plus a DC fight on SDA (tens of mA, a guess). Corrected severity: low.

**Bench.** Dev op on a spare board: bit-bang into the state a reset leaves, hold it under 100 ms, read `palReadLine(SDA)`, the SDA voltage and the INA228's current step, try three PCF reads, then free the bus.

**Fix.** Add the bus clear before the first transaction and after a failure. Better: read the arbitration with SDA released to an input pull-up, and idle SDA as an input pull-up instead of driven high, so a slave holding it low never meets a driven high.

### F-048 · low · The SPI0 dispatcher swallows a FIFO interrupt when a stale DMA flag is set

`lib/chibios-contrib/os/hal/ports/SN32/LLD/SN32F2xx/SPI/hal_spi_v2_lld.c:352` · needs-hardware · severity unchanged · audit #16 · found by lcd-bus-spi

**What is wrong.** `SN32_SPI0_HANDLER` routes to `sn32_flash_dma_isr()` whenever DMAHTIF or DMATCIF is in RIS, and that function always returns true, so the FIFO handler is skipped (`hal_spi_v2_lld.c:352`, `:337`). Its TC branch also runs the whole teardown without checking `sn32_dma_busy` (`:306`). The board's own finding (`docs/sn32f299.md:906-909`) is that an IC write drops the pending request. If that is right, a FIFO interrupt taken with a stale DMA flag in RIS clears only the DMA bit and is never raised again: a driver `spiSend()` (tx8, used by `set_madctl()` at the animation toggle, `graphics/lcd_bus.c:1436`) waits forever and the watchdog resets the board. The hazard is already recorded as open (`keyboards/a_jazz/ak820pro/PATCHES.md:74-80`, `plans/FIRMWARE-FINDINGS-2026-09-23.md:179`, `:247`).

**Test:** /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-016/test_spi0_dispatch.c (with regmodel.c, regmodel.h, stubs/hal.h, run.sh; the candidate fix on a scratch copy is fix/hal_spi_v2_lld.c, run by run_fix.sh)

```sh
sh /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-016/run.sh; sh /Users/jdlien/code/ak820pro-audit-20261031/verify-016/run_fix.sh
```

Result: the real `hal_spi_v2_lld.c` with the SPI registers on trapping pages, under two interrupt models. One entry with a stale DMATCIF and a FIFO request leaves the transfer unfinished in both. Under a level-sensitive line the next entry completes it; under the edge-like model the tx8 never completes and a polled fill is refused (a repaint). The fixed copy passes every scenario under both models.

**Why it needs the bench.** It needs a DMATCIF that latches late, after the rescue's IC write, and an edge-like interrupt line; both are unmeasured. Run E5's 417 rescues with no reset in 3 hours rule out one sub-case. The remaining exposure today is Fn+Delete with a provisioned GIF right after a rescued blit, and both boards' animation slots are empty.

**Bench.** (1) Latch DMATCIF deliberately, hand back as an abort does but without its IC write, `spiStartSendI` one byte, `spiSynchronizeS` with 50 ms: a timeout means edge-like. (2) Counters for a late DMATCIF after a `!DMAEN` rescue and for DMA-path entries with no transfer of ours; an hour of crash_hunt with the seconds clock.

**Fix.** In `sn32_flash_dma_isr()`, right after `SN_SPI0->IC = ris;`, `if (!sn32_dma_busy) return false;`, which falls through to the FIFO handler. The last change to this dispatch regressed on silicon (the reverted `2a17a73b48`), so hunt it on the board before merging.

- **lens-power-loss.** Read in full: `kb_eeconfig.c/.h`, `flash_tidy.c/.h`, `flash_stats.c/.h`, `rtc/rtc_persist.c/.h`, `menu/profiles.c/.h`, `menu_glue.c`, QMK's wear leveling and its EFL backing store, the SN32F290 EFL LLD, the EEPROM glue, `eeconfig.c`, `nvm_eeconfig.c`, `nvm_dynamic_keymap.c`, the external-flash write policy and the flash channel. Skimmed `power.c`. Not covered: the RAM-only records and the PCF writes.

## Loose ends noticed in passing

Not findings, but worth a look.

- **Dev op 17 sub 4 is not clamped.** `hid_protocol.c:1238` computes `data[5] * 100` ms, and its comment says "at most 5 s", but nothing clamps it. `ch582_raw81_stall()` then waits up to 25.5 s inside the HID handler, past the 12 s watchdog. Dev builds only. (Checked in the source for this report; fuzz-raw81's notes say it clamps, which is wrong.)
- **SN32 serial LLD.** On a break, the UART ISR clears the receive-line interrupt enable permanently and exits before serving THRE; its `while (ls & RDR)` drain is unbounded at priority 0. An overrun that appears between two LS reads is cleared without an overrun flag (a sub-microsecond window).
- **SN32 USB LLD.** The `INSTS == 0` branch returns without clearing anything; `ERR_TIMEOUT` has no handler if it is ever enabled; and the stale-SOF undo of a suspend (F-045).
- **Stale comments and docs.** `rtc/rtc.h:25-27` and `:54-56` (the seed retry, F-006); `menu/profiles.c:708-714` (sub 5 and the face bit); `lcd_bus.c`'s `ANIM_ROOM` "= 244" (it is 243) and `docs/display.md:697`; the I2C timeout comment says 20 ms where the code uses 10; `menu/menu.h:35` on the Pairing backstop; `lockchan.h:88` names a `lockchan_last()` that does not exist.
- **Test scaffolding.** `scripts/diag_sim/stubs_kbee/graphics/display.h` says `DISPLAY_CLOCK_MODE_COUNT 4`; the firmware has 6. `tests/watchdog_record_test.c` targets the ds-phase API and does not build on this branch.
- **Row ISR cost.** `rgb_callback` re-evaluates `current_row % 3` with a software divide once per column, 15 divides per row ISR (roughly 10-15 µs of the ~160 µs body). Upstream; hoisting it out of the loop is cheap if ISR time ever matters.
- **`spi_lld_start()`'s** if/if/else would assert for SPID0 with both SPI instances enabled; harmless only because assertions are off.
- **Weekday.** After a step, `rtc_set_time_ms` stores ChibiOS's 1-7 (Monday = 1) where the host sends 0-6, so later PCF writes and GET replies carry 1-7. Nothing on the board shows the weekday.
- **Rename's DRAIN** swallows new presses until every key is up; documented and intended, but a user who commits with Enter and keeps typing loses those keys.
- **`HC_PROFILES` sub 1** drops a pending active-slot choice before it refuses; negligible in practice.
