AK820 Config Menu

The knob menu as it would look on the board's 128 × 128 screen, drawn pixel for pixel from the fonts the board actually stores. Press Fn + click to close it and see the dashboard; press it again to reopen.

Painting

Board speed paints one glyph per main-loop pass, about 215 a second, the way the board's pump does. Drawn shapes (the triangle, the bars) go out at once. Lit and inverted text need a font path the board doesn't have yet: a 1-bit copy of the small font in the chip's own flash. Judge the grey and the yellow on the panel itself, since its greys read darker than a monitor's.

Saved on the board

Turning previews a value straight away. Only a click saves it, which is one flash write. Holding, or closing the menu mid-edit, puts the old value back.

What the panel allows

  • Today each glyph is copied straight from the font in flash, white on black, so text can't be coloured or inverted.
  • A 1-bit copy of the small font in the chip's own flash (about 1 KB) would change that: the firmware could draw any glyph in any two colours from RAM, which makes inverted and yellow text possible.
  • What a cursor costs is repainting. The triangle moves by redrawing two tiny shapes. Lit and inverted rows repaint two whole rows, up to 40 glyphs, on every detent: about 0.2 s at board speed. Scrolling by line repaints all 8 rows, about 0.7 s.
  • The small face (Cozette, 6 × 14) gives a title and 8 rows of 20 characters; the triangle takes one of them. The big face (10 × 23) fits 12 across, for values.
  • While the menu is open the screen keeps at least level 1, so turning its brightness to 0 can't hide the menu. It turns off when the menu closes.
