## The short version

- **The MCU cannot see the pack.** The SN32 can measure only its own supply,
  VDD, and on battery that is a buck-boost's output: ~3.90 V whatever the pack
  is doing. No analogue pin reaches the pack either.
- **The Bluetooth module can.** The CH582F sits on the unregulated rail and
  reports a byte, `5C`, that everyone took for a (fake) percentage. It is a
  **linear voltmeter on the pack**: `5C = 114.22 × V − 361.04`.
- **Averaged, it agrees with a meter to within about the meter's resolution:**
  eight checks on the 09-28 run, 4.01 V down to 3.22 V, all within 11 mV
  (below).
- **The level is a fraction of runtime**, read off a curve of pack voltage.
  The voltage is good to ~10 mV. The curve is **fitted on two drain tests**
  (Phase 1b's refit). Scored each run against the other, it is within
  0.54-0.59 points rms below 3.95 V, and 3.1-3.2 rms (worst ±8.9) on the
  3.95-4.036 V plateau. That is one unit, one load, a fresh pack.
- **Over a whole cycle (2026-10-09 to 10-11)** it stayed within ±2.5 points
  from 52% to the cut, but dipped up to **8.3 points low** on the curve's flat
  top (4003-4016 mV spans 70-85%). **The next cycle (10-12 to 10-14)** was
  within 4.6 points low from 52% to the cut and up to **7.2 low** on the flat
  top, erring low throughout (never more than 0.4 high). So ±5% holds
  everywhere but 70-85%.
- **Against the logger's measured energy (10-15 to 10-18)** it was worse on
  the plateau: up to **13.7 points low** at 70-80% (mean −10.1), means of
  −1.9 to −4.9 from 60% to 20%, within 2.5 below 20%. The plateau moves ~1 mV
  a point, so `5C`'s ~10 mV and a cycle's drift each cost ~10 points there:
  Phase 2 counts energy across it instead.
- **It is blind at both ends:** above ~4.036 V (the top ~10% of runtime) and
  below ~3.161 V. While charging, the charge current inflates the reading, so
  **the charging display is a model** of the charge, bounded by the voltage,
  not a reading (Phase 1b, below).
- ⚠️ **macOS's Bluetooth battery is not this gauge.** System Settings shows the
  module's own linear `5C`, which reads far higher (96% against 66.4% on
  10-10).

## Where the pack voltage comes from: `5C`

### What `5C` is

The CH582F sends the MCU a `5C <byte>` frame: the stock firmware's battery
percentage, and the only battery signal the stock firmware had (none of the
nine stock SN32F290 images in `fpb/ajazz-ak820-pro` — AK820 Pro v1.13, v1.14
and seven sibling boards — references the ADC, comparator or op-amp base
addresses). It is not a cell model. It is a straight line on the pack voltage,
fitted against a meter at five settled points on the 09-25 discharge
(`history/battery-2026-09-25/readings.csv`: 3.84 → 78, 3.81 → 74, 3.74 → 66,
3.68 → 59, 3.30 → 16):

    5C = 114.22 × V_pack − 361.04      r² = 0.99987, residuals < 0.5 count
    inverted:  V_pack = (5C + 361.04) / 114.22

- **One count is 8.75 mV.**
- **It clamps:** 100 above ~4.036 V and 0 below ~3.161 V. Both ends are the
  line extrapolated, not measured. A full pack rests at 4.18 V, above the
  ceiling, so `5C` reads 100 for the first ~5 hours of a discharge at full
  white — which is why every test before 09-26 concluded "it only ever says
  100": they all ran inside the clamp.
- **It reads terminal voltage.** Plugged in near full it snaps to 100 (the
  charger's CV voltage is above the clamp); plugged in flat it climbs slowly
  (4 → 100 over 3 h 55 min on 09-28). See "Charging", below.
- **A board with no pack fitted reports 0** (`indicators.c` uses that, with
  the charger pins, for "No Batt").
- **Fitted on one unit, on discharge, 3.30-3.84 V.** Checked since across a
  recharge and up to 4.01 V (below). Another unit's module may sit on a
  different line.

### How often it arrives

Three observations, which do not yet add up to a mechanism:

- the firmware sends `A6 53` every 5 s (`CH582_BATTERY_POLL_MS`), as the stock
  firmware does;
- the battery log counts **224-237 reports per 10-minute entry** on 09-28
  (log v3) and **262-334** on 09-29/30 (v4): one per ~1.8-2.7 s;
- the debug page's age (the `3` in `62@3`) **counts up to ~4 and resets**
  (JD, 2026-09-30 ~20:00): gaps of ~4-5 s.

So **2-3 reports per 5-second poll cycle, bunched together** rather than spread
evenly. ⚠️ With the lights cut (10-01, from ~02:13) the count fell to
**194-256 per entry**, some entries with every report identical — which fits
the module also sending a report when the reading changes. A hint, not a
finding. Whether that is several replies to one poll, or the reply plus an
unprompted stream, has not been checked (a capture of the serial frames would show
it). It matters for one thing: if a bunch repeats one measurement, the 64
reports the estimate averages are ~25 independent readings, not 64.

Flash 1c's per-call profile (2026-10-07) saw the module answer each 5 s poll
with **three reports in one burst**, parsed in one main-loop pass. That is
how the old sort below cost ~6 ms of one pass on battery. Whether the three
are one measurement repeated is still unchecked.

### From reports to millivolts

The CH582F driver hands every report to `battery_5c_report()` as it is parsed
— never the cached last value, which would count one report many times.

- **The estimate is a trimmed mean of the last 64 reports**, in hundredths of
  a count: drop the highest and lowest eighth, average the rest. That
  spans ~2-2.5 min at the current report rate. At least 5 reports before there
  is an estimate at all.
- **By counting, not sorting** (Phase 1b's D, since flash 2). It used to
  insertion-sort the ring on every report: ~1.75 ms a report on the board
  (worst 3.6), ~6 ms for a poll's burst of three. That was the battery-only
  ≥ 10 ms stall: on USB at the clamp every report is 100 and the sort moved
  nothing. A histogram of the ring's values, kept on every insert, eviction
  and forget, gives the same sum over the same order. It matched the sort
  exactly on 99,972 estimates (`scripts/battery_sim`, `c5_equivalence`).
- **Why a mean and not a median** (the median of 7 before `deef6053dd`): a
  median of whole counts is a whole count, and one count is 1.2-5% of level
  depending on where the pack is, so the level moved in lumps and then sat
  still for an hour. A single report jitters by ±2-3 counts (the log's
  per-period min/max), and it dithers between neighbouring counts, so the mean
  resolves about a tenth of a count (the log's means read like `62.98`). The
  trim keeps a median's indifference to a stray report.
- **Emptied** when no report has arrived for 20 s, at every supply change, and
  whenever the charger starts or stops: reports taken under charge current do
  not describe the pack without it, and vice versa.
- **The clamps:** an estimate ≥ 99.50 is the top clamp (the pack is only known
  to be ≥ 4036 mV) and < 0.50 is the bottom one (≤ 3161 mV). Those are bounds,
  never fed to the curve; the debug page prints them as `>4036` and `<3161`.
- **Millivolts:** `(estimate + 361.04) × 1000 / 114.22`, in integers
  (`c5_to_mv`).

## What the level means

⭐ **The fraction of runtime left, at the load the curve was measured at.**
Behind a buck-boost the board draws **constant power**, so the pack current
rises as the voltage falls (about 27% between 4.18 and 3.30 V). A timed run at
fixed settings therefore calibrates *runtime*, not charge: at total runtime `T`
the level at any voltage is `remaining / T`. That is also what a user wants to
know. Calling it state of charge would need a current measurement.

**The curve is the whole accuracy question now.** The voltage is good to ~10 mV;
how much level 10 mV is worth depends on the curve's slope, and on this pack
that swings widely: **~0.3% per 10 mV near empty, 1-6% through the middle,
and ~11% on the 4.00-4.02 V plateau**, where ~20% of the runtime passes in
~20 mV.

- ⚠️ **The textbook NMC rest-voltage curve does not fit this pack.** It puts
  3.74 V at ~47%; on 09-25 the pack reached 3.74 V at 49.3 h and was dead by
  63.4 h, so it had **at most ~25%** of its runtime left.
- ⭐ **The curve on the board: the refit on both runs** (Phase 1b's A,
  `scripts/battery_fit.py --refit`, since flash 1 on 2026-10-05;
  `curve_mv`/`curve_pm` in `battery.c`; output in
  [`history/battery-2026-10-01-drain/refit-output.txt`](../history/battery-2026-10-01-drain/refit-output.txt)).
  Each knot is the mean of the two runs' smoothed voltages at that fraction of
  runtime: run 1's 51.55 h and run 2's 50.41 h of equivalent full white (50.56
  h on the wall; the difference credits one dump plug-in).

## The gauge as built, Phase 1 (`deef6053dd`)

> Phase 1b (the next section) builds on this. It replaced the charging display,
> the re-seat's timing, the warning's threshold and re-arm, the countdown's
> rate and the log's format, and each statement it superseded is marked here
> rather than deleted. Everything unmarked is still how the board works.

### Supply: what powers the board

A state machine on the charger's CHRG pin and VDD, because a VDD threshold
cannot tell "unplugged" from "plugged in and charging hard" (the old 4300 mV
predicate called a pack charging at VDD 4193 mV "on battery" for four hours on
09-28, and flickered in the taper):

    CHRG low on 2 ticks              -> USB, held 1 s past the last low
                                        (covers the no-pack ~2 Hz pulse)
    raw VDD >= 4100 mV for 0.5 s     -> USB
    raw VDD <  4000 mV for 1 s,
      with no evidence of USB        -> battery
    otherwise                        -> unchanged

On battery VDD is ~3.90 V; on USB no raw sample went under 4119 mV across a
7.5 h charge into a dead pack. ⚠️ The ADC reference is ±2% per part: re-check
both margins on another unit.

### The level

Kept internally in tenths of a percent.

- **On battery, below the top clamp:** the curve at the pack voltage, through
  **the ratchet** — it never rises; it moves down only after **six fresh
  estimates in a row** sit below it, and then to the *highest* of the six, at
  most 1% per move. Observed: ~15 s per 1% step (the 09-29 post-flash walk).
- **On battery, at the top clamp:** the voltage says only "≥ 4.036 V", so the
  level **counts down by time** from wherever charging left it: 1% per ~32 min
  (the 09-25 clamp span), floored at **90.5%**, just above the clamp's exit at
  90%, so a whole-percent or tenths panel runs into the curve without a step.
  *Rate superseded (Phase 1b's E4):* `COUNTDOWN_S_PER_PM` is 184 s per 0.1%,
  the mean of run 1's 185.5 and run 2's 181.5, so 1% per ~31 min. From FULL
  the countdown reaches its floor in ~4.86 h.
- **Booted at the top clamp** (every cable ↔ BT slider flip reboots): the saved
  level if it is in the clamp's 90-100 band, else a guess of 95. Below the
  clamp nothing is restored; the voltage is the authority.
- **Charging** reads the curve **150 mV below** the terminal voltage
  (`CHARGE_IR_MV`, the charge current's I×R: ~0.8-0.9 A through ~180 mΩ, a
  guess, unmeasured), only rises, and only in a **real charging session** (CHRG
  low for 60 s, so a brief plug-in cannot bump it). It stops at **97.0%**
  (creeping there through the CV phase at the clamp), and says **100 only when
  the charger terminates**: CHRG released for 10 s, on USB, with `5C` at 100.
  Charging termination with the board running in BT on USB is verified
  (09-28: a top-up released at 15:43, the gauge went to 100).
  **Superseded (Phase 1b's B, flash 2):** on the fitted curve this read ~45%
  3.8 h into a charge from flat, then raced to ~90% once `5C` clamped. Charging
  now shows a time model bounded by the voltage, and `CHARGE_IR_MV` is gone.
  FULL's rule (CHRG released 10 s, on USB, `5C` at the clamp) and the 60 s
  session are unchanged.
- **The re-seat:** after a real charging session the level is taken straight
  from the first post-unplug estimate below the clamp, once, so a wrong I×R
  costs one correction at unplug rather than hours of a wrong level.
  **Superseded (Phase 1b):** it now waits `RELAX_S` (30 min) for a relaxed
  pack, and has a rule for an unplug at the clamp.
- **The saved level** (kb eeconfig, 0.5% steps) is written at FULL, then at
  97.5 / 95 / 92.5 / 90 on battery (rounded down, so a restore never
  overstates), and once below the band, so a stale 9x is not restored after a
  pack swap: **six writes a charge cycle**, none while charging. It was every
  whole-percent change (~100 a cycle) until `deef6053dd`.
  ⚠️ **Every internal-flash write blanks the whole LED matrix for a few ms**
  (the longest flash-marked main-loop interval measured is 7 ms; the blank's own
  duration is unmeasured): the row ISR deliberately de-selects every row while
  the flash driver is in its program state ([leds.md](leds.md) item 3). Interrupts themselves are masked
  only for each per-line program window (tens of µs; `hal_efl_lld.c`), because
  the vector table and ISRs live in flash and the Cortex-M0 has no VTOR; a
  row left energized across one would flash at ~18×. Page ERASE (1-2 ms per
  1 KB page, datasheet) is left unmasked to protect the CH582F's UART. JD
  sees the blank as a brief whole-board blink; a brightness step reproduces it.
  (An earlier version of this paragraph said interrupts are masked for the
  whole write. They are not.) Count them with
  `ak820health.py --stalls --json` (`flash_writes`; since Phase 1b also by
  writer and by field, `HC_FLASHW`). The milestones were never the frequent
  writer: Phase 1b's C found the clock's (below).

Queued fixes (the plan, "Queued"): show "Charge", not the 95 guess, while
charging at the clamp with nothing saved; don't spend the re-seat on a clamp
reading just after unplug — on 09-29 that left the level at 95 and the ratchet
walked it to ~86 at 1% per ~15 s. **Both done in Phase 1b** (flash 2):
UNKNOWN-CHG shows "Charge", and the re-seat waits `RELAX_S`.

### Protection

On the **pack voltage** from `5C` (it compared VDD until `8627554513`, and VDD
never fell far enough to fire):

- **"Battery low"** once, after 30 s under **3550 mV**; re-armed above 3650 mV
  or on USB. **Superseded (Phase 1b's E1/E2, flash 1):** the default is
  **3680 mV, the curve's 10%**, ~5 h before the cut at full white. It re-arms
  only after a **real charging session** (CHRG low 60 s), or on a new
  threshold over `HC_BATTCFG` (so it can be tested on demand), never on a
  voltage.
- **"Low: RGB off"** after 30 s under **3400 mV**. Three fresh reports of 0 in
  a row cut at once and set the level to 0 ("Low").
- The cut is a **power cap** (`power_set_lights_cut`), not an RGB toggle, so
  the user's own `Fn`+`X` cannot undo it. It lifts only after **5 s of fresh
  evidence of USB** (CHRG low or VDD read high that second, not a remembered
  state). A stale reading never clears it.
- No threshold is judged at the top clamp, where the pack is only known to be
  ≥ 4036 mV.
- Thresholds are RAM-only, in pack mV, 0 disables: `ak820battery.py cfg WARN
  CUT`. A reboot restores the defaults.
- This sheds load; it does not disconnect the cell. The pack's own protection
  circuit does that (the 09-25 run: 0 V at the connector by 09-28 02:59).
- ✅ **Gate 2 passed on hardware**, the 09-28 run's last night: "Battery low"
  at ~00:00 on 10-01 (3544 mV on the debug row); the cut at ~02:13 (from the
  log's LED drive); JD could not turn the LEDs back on; 5 s of USB lifted it;
  and ~1 min after unplugging, still under 3400 mV, it cut again. ⚠️ Every
  plug-in re-arms the warning, so each brief dump re-fires it after unplugging.
  (Fixed by the re-arm rule above: a dump shorter than 60 s is no session.)

### The log (v4; v5 since flash 2, in the next section)

**720 ten-minute entries, 120 h, in RAM.** Each holds VDD mean and min; every
`5C` report of the period as sum, count (16-bit), min and max; flags at the end
**and** OR-ed over the period, so a transition cannot fall between entries; the
level in 0.5%; the mean effective LED drive (`led_pm`, after the power cap);
and the effective LCD backlight. The reply carries the format version and the
period; the host tool never assumes either.

```sh
set -o pipefail
venv/bin/python3 hostagent/ak820battery.py log out.csv   # dump
venv/bin/python3 hostagent/ak820battery.py               # state (HC_CONN)
```

⚠️ **The log dies with the board.** A flat pack, a flash, and a **BT → cable
slider flip** (a reset) each lose it. Dump with the **slider on BT**, and
unplug within a minute: longer is a charging session, which re-seats the level
and charges the pack. ⚠️ A loose cable charges without enumerating: check for
USB id `0C45`.

`HC_CONN` (raw-HID channel 0x13) carries the live fields: VDD, charger pins,
the rounded estimate, its age, flags, the level, pack mV, LED drive, state and
the format version (`hid_protocol.c` has the byte map).

### Charging: why a model

While charging, `5C` reads the terminal voltage, which the charge current lifts
above the pack's own. The discharge curve's 4.00-4.02 V plateau has no
counterpart on charge, where the terminal voltage climbs steadily, so **no
constant offset maps a charging voltage onto the curve.** Phase 1's
`curve(V − 150 mV)` read ~45% 3.8 h into a charge from flat (10-01), then raced
to ~90% once `5C` clamped.

So the charging level is an **empirical model of a charge from JD's Mac's USB
port, in the BT position, through the ASC4056**: an estimate for that stated
condition, not a measurement. Its quantity is the gauge's own, the fraction of
full-white runtime. A different charger, a hot pack or a hub that cannot
supply the current is outside what it claims. JD chose (2026-10-08, the plan's
F3 option A) to show the model's number while charging, rather than "Charge"
for every session until the knee is measured.

### The model

`battery.c` ("Charging: the model"); its constants are in `battery.h` and the
generated `battery_tail.h`. All in per mille of full (pm), integer, once a
second, counting only seconds the charger is charging.

- **Linear to the knee:** `M = L0 + K_CC·t`, with `K_CC` **133 pm/h**, until
  `M` reaches `L_KNEE`, **533 pm**. From 0 that is ~4.0 h.
- **Then the tail:** `M = A − (A − L_KNEE)·e^(−t/τ)`, a decelerating approach
  that starts at `K_CC` (never faster than the linear phase) and reaches 990
  exactly `T_TAIL` after the knee. `T_TAIL` is **313 min** (18,780 s), τ 5.78
  h, `A` 1301.8 pm; it ends at ~54 pm/h. It is compiled in as a table, one
  node a minute in 1/16 pm, not iterated. A start above the knee joins the
  tail at its own level.
- **Where the numbers came from:**
  - `K_CC`: three partial charges on hardware (B3, below);
  - `T_TAIL`: the median of two charges from flat, VDD's rise to termination
    (5.28 h on 09-28, 5.17 h on 10-01);
  - `L_KNEE`: 133 pm/h × 4.235 h (plug-in to VDD's rise) − 30 pm (an assumed
    refill of the reserve below 0%). An extrapolation, stated as one.

**The displayed level, each second**, by precedence:
1. It never falls while charging (until FULL, or "Charge").
2. It never rises past the evidence. With a fresh estimate below the clamp it
   rises at most to `U = curve(estimate)`, the voltage ceiling. `U` overstates
   under charge: a ceiling, never a target. At the clamp there is no voltage
   bound, and it advances on elapsed charging time alone. With no fresh
   estimate it does not rise.
3. It rises at most 1 pm a second, so a lifted ceiling shows as a quick
   count-up and the percent never skips a value, except at FULL.
4. It never exceeds 990 (99%) before FULL.

**FULL** keeps Phase 1's rule (CHRG released 10 s, on USB, `5C` at the clamp)
and shows 100: a step of a few points (2.6 on gate 8's charge from 0). A 100
already shown stays 100 through a top-up.

| state | when | the panel |
|---|---|---|
| FROM-FLAT | a boot on USB whose first estimate (its first five reports) is ≤ 10.00 counts, ≤ 3248 mV under charge; then a real session | 0, then the model |
| MODEL | a real session (CHRG low 60 s) from a known level | the model |
| UNKNOWN-CHG | charging with no trusted start: a boot on USB above that, a reboot mid-charge, or a resumption after a handover that adopted nothing | "Charge" |
| LOST | the model contradicted, below | "Charge" until FULL, an unplug or a handover |
| FULL | the charger terminated | 100 |

**LOST is a consistency check**, not a qualification of a supply. It catches
large, sustained contradictions only:
- (a) `M` more than 50 pm above a fresh below-clamp `U`, for 30 min
  accumulated (the clamp and missing reports hold the timer, they do not reset
  it);
- (b) no fresh estimate for 10 min;
- (c) OVERRUN: 60 min of charging after the tail clock reaches `T_TAIL`,
  without FULL.

**A pause** (the charger stops while still on USB) holds the display and every
timer. One that lasts `PAUSE_S` (30 min) is a **handover**: the model closes,
and the idle path adopts a level once from a relaxed estimate, the re-seat's
rule below. A charge that resumes after it requalifies at 60 s.

### The re-seat at unplug

After a real session, the level is corrected once on battery, from the first
estimate built only from reports received at least **`RELAX_S`, 1800 s (30
min)**, after the unplug:
- **below the clamp:** the curve at that estimate, up or down, once. It is the
  one upward move ever allowed on battery, and it corrects whatever the model
  got wrong;
- **at the clamp:** a known level is lifted to 90.5% if it was below; an
  unknown one takes the clamp's 95% guess. A LOST session re-seats as unknown.

Until it resolves the level holds, though the clamp countdown still runs. A
replug before it keeps it owed, and `RELAX_S` restarts at the next unplug.
⚠️ **After unplugging an UNKNOWN or LOST session the row is blank for those 30
min**: an accepted cost (gate 7, finding 3).

**Why 30 min:** just after an unplug the pack's voltage is still relaxing, so
the level it implies reads high. Trial 1 measured ~1 point high at 6-10 min,
~0.6 at 15 and ~0.3 at 30; the 10-08 recharge ~1.7 at 1-2 min (the plan's
first guess was 120 s). Over three relaxations, at 30 min it sits 0.1-0.35
point above its 60 min value.

### Internal-flash writes: the blink, and how few are left

Every internal-flash write blinks the LEDs dark for a few ms. The cause is the
row ISR's guard, not interrupt masking ([leds.md](leds.md)). Phase 1b counted
the writes by writer and by field (C1: `HC_FLASHW`, shown by
`ak820health.py --stalls`).

- **C1 named the writer** on flash 1's first battery soak (19.1 h, 10-05/06):
  12 write sessions. Six were stores of the clock's divider period (of 27
  proposals), on its PCF path; five were level milestones; one was an LCD
  brightness change. RGB, VIA and other wrote nothing. On battery that path
  saved 6-8 times a day: its ≥ 64-tick threshold did not stop temperature
  wander from rewriting it.
- **C2 (flash 2):** `rtc/rtc_persist.c` owns every save of the period. The
  correction paths still correct the live clock; they only propose. The first
  save comes at 10 min of uptime if nothing is stored. Later ones need the
  candidate ≥ 64 ticks off **and** ≥ 6 h since this boot's last save: ≤ 4 a
  day ([clock.md](clock.md)).
- **Gate 8's 24 h soak (10-09/10): 8 write sessions.** Five were the level
  milestones and three were period stores (of 29 proposals): 3 outside the
  milestones, against the ≤ 10 a day the plan allowed.
- **What is left:** the level's milestones (≤ 6 a charge cycle: FULL, 97.5,
  95, 92.5, 90 and one marker below the band), ≤ 4 period saves a day, and
  JD's own setting changes.
- **A flash costs far more than a day of use:** on 10-05 a flash and
  `flash.sh`'s restores came to 787 VIA write sessions and 8 whole-store
  erases.

### Endurance

The SN32F299 datasheet gives **20,000 erase+program cycles minimum, 100,000
typical**, on 1 KB pages. The emulated EEPROM is QMK's wear-leveling over a
2048-byte store (1024 logical bytes): a log of 8-byte slots, erased whole only
when it fills. Over gate 8's 24 h soak the board's own writes made 16 program
operations and no erase. ⚠️ **The plan's C3, the years to 20,000 cycles, has
not been computed.**

### The level after a flash

A flash wipes RAM and the emulated EEPROM. **On battery the level comes
straight back from the pack voltage** (1.5% within seconds of the 15:39 flash
on 10-11), or the 95% guess at the top clamp. **On USB power it is unknown**:
the charger lifts the voltage and the model has no start, so the row says
"Charge" until FULL, or until an unplug plus `RELAX_S`. Owners rarely flash;
two low-priority fixes are in [`plans/BACKLOG.md`](../plans/BACKLOG.md) ("The
level after a flash").

### macOS shows a different percent

**macOS's Bluetooth battery (System Settings) is the CH582F's own `5C`**, a
straight line on the pack voltage served over Bluetooth's battery service, not
this gauge. It reads high most of the way down: 96% against the gauge's 66.4%
at 03:50 10-10, and, computed from the line and the curve, ~59% at the gauge's
10% and ~27% at the RGB cut. System Settings reads it live; `system_profiler`
shows a stale cached value. Our firmware reaches the module only over UART, and
no known command sets its figure. [`plans/BACKLOG.md`](../plans/BACKLOG.md)
("macOS shows the module's battery percent") has the three routes.

### The log (v5)

Since flash 2 each of the 720 ten-minute entries is 18 bytes: v4's fields plus

  runs);
- **`chg`**: the charging state at the end (bits 0-2), and, latched over the

## The gauge as built, Phase 2: the energy count (`d3e8389858`)

The plan, its three codex reviews and every disposition:
[`plans/PHASE2-BUILD-PLAN.md`](../plans/PHASE2-BUILD-PLAN.md). The evidence:
R1-R4 and A1-A8 in [`plans/POWER-CAMPAIGN-PLAN.md`](../plans/POWER-CAMPAIGN-PLAN.md).

**Why:** on the curve's flat stretches a few mV are ~10 points. Against the
logger's measured energy (R3) the voltage-read level was up to 13.8 points low
at 70-80%, and a refit cannot help: the plateau moved 10-25 mV between
cycles. The board is a constant-power load (305-311 mW over R3's whole drain),
so its energy can be counted.

**The count.** On battery, each second, the board's modelled draw over the
elapsed time comes off the level, one per mille per 56,084.4 mW.s (15,579 mWh,
R3's energy from FULL to the RGB cut, `E_CAP_MWH`). The model (`battery.c`,
"The energy count"), in mW at ~4.17 V:
- the base, every light out: 106.9 (R1's 114.0 less R2's USB data);
- USB data, while USB is configured on battery (the bench only): +7.1;
- the backlight: 39.1 x its duty / 48 (linear, R1);
- the RGB: 9.5 while any LED is lit, plus 2.857 uW per unit of the channel
  sum (Phase 2's single slope, superseded by the per-colour model below,
  "The RGB model is per colour"), from **the committed frames, time-weighted** (the LED driver sums each
  frame it hands the row ISR and keeps sum x ms and lit ms:
  `sn32f2xx_led_drive_take`). Linear in drive; QMK's CIE curve makes it look
  otherwise in values (value 64 and 128 are drives of 12 and 48).
Calibrated for JD's unit as it runs (BT linked, the MCU awake, the panel
drawing). Not measured: the radio's other states, 2.4G, temperature.

**The voltage bounds it, by a band, asymmetric** (`BATTERY_BAND_MV_UP` 25,
`_DOWN` 40, `_PM` 15 in `battery.h`):
- above `curve(V + 25) + 15` the level is ratcheted down (6 estimates in a
  row, at most 1% a step); an allowance reaching the clamp's 4036 mV is no
  bound;
- below `curve(V − 40) − 15` the level waits and the count's energy is
  dropped, not banked; only with a fresh estimate. The pack's voltage rises
  when the load falls (R1: +10 mV from 73 to 28 mA), so a tighter lower edge
  would pause the count through every idle night;
- at the top clamp nothing bounds it (no 90.5% floor any more: a light load
  stays clamped below 90% true).

**0% is the cut.** No level on battery goes below 1% on its own (the count,
the first estimate, every adoption, the entry to battery); the protection's
cut latching sets 0, which wins over any re-seat and hides time left.

**Anchors:** FULL sets 100% and restarts the count; after a charge the count
runs from the charging model's level through the 30-minute relaxation, and
the re-seat keeps it inside the band (on the plateau the band is wide, so the
model's level stands; where the curve is steep the voltage moves it). On idle
USB a known level stays, bounded by the band. A reboot on battery starts from
the voltage (or, clamped, the saved level: a 90-100% point or "under 90", 89%):
its accuracy is the voltage's, not claimed.

**Time left** (`battery_time_left_min`): the level's energy over the average
draw, which is the mean of the minutes on battery so far and an exponential
average with a 12 h time constant from the 720th; frozen on USB. On
`Fn`+`D`'s camera page: `58% 31h`, `85% 4d`, `9% <1h`, `100%~51h` while under
an hour of average; nothing on USB, unknown or after the cut.

**Measured on the host** (`scripts/battery_sim`, 121 cases): R3 and drain 2e
replayed through the firmware at one-second steps, under three orderings of
each 10-minute entry's `5C` reports: **within +0.1 / +1.2 points at every
second**, against the gauge's −13.8 and −7.3. With the capacity deliberately
wrong (`run.sh --sensitivity`): ±5% stays within 3.8 points; an aged pack
(the firmware assuming 13% more than the pack holds) reads up to 5.8 high; a
model over-counting by 13% reads up to 10.2 low. **Not yet measured: a drain
under the ladder's changing load (R5).**

**Raw HID:** `HC_POWER` (0x0F; `ak820battery.py` prints it): the mode and
stage, the count's state, the draw and its average, time left, the energy since
FULL, the band. **The log, v6:** 15-minute entries (180 h), the period's
modelled energy in `vdd_min`'s place.

## The gauge across a deep sleep (the deep-sleep build)

The design and its five codex rounds:
[`plans/DEEP-SLEEP-BUILD-PLAN.md`](../plans/DEEP-SLEEP-BUILD-PLAN.md),
sections 4 and 6. **Why it needs anything:** QMK's timer stands still asleep
(the system timer is stopped), so a count driven by elapsed time would simply
lose the sleep. The slept time comes from the PCF ([clock.md](clock.md),
"Across a deep sleep") and is handed over explicitly (`battery.h`):

- **`battery_sleep_enter()`**, just before the lock: the awake energy
  integrated up to the sleep, and the log's state noted (flags, level,
  charging state, the open period's start).
- **`battery_sleep_wake()`**, at the wake, before any other battery work:
  the open period frozen, the live intake reset (the radio's `5C` after the
  wake lands there), the `5C` estimate forgotten (reports from before the
  sleep never pass for fresh), and `HC_BATTLOG` pending.
- **`battery_sleep_account(ms)`**, once the slept time is known: the sleep's
  energy at **`BATTERY_P_SLEEP_UW` = 13,350 µW** (3.24 mA at 4.12 V on the
  logger, the hour asleep of 10-19; assumed constant power) comes off the
  level and goes into the energy since FULL, **never into the average**; the
  relaxation after an unplug is aged by the slept time (the pack relaxed
  while the board slept); the log's entries are laid in; time left is
  recomputed at once, for the wake's message. A sleep on USB power costs the
  pack nothing.

**Time left is hours of use.** The average is the awake draw's, so time left
says how long the board can be *used*: asleep at 13.35 mW against 107-300
awake, the sleeps stretch the calendar, not the hours. The battery row shows
it under 10 hours (`18% 6h`), and a wake after 2 h or more asleep says `6h use
left` once ([display.md](display.md)).

**The log, v7.** Each 15-minute entry carries **the seconds it spent asleep**
(`slept_s`, in `vdd_avg`'s place: on battery that was the buck-boost's 3.90 V,
and the supply is in the flags anyway). The log keeps its own time base,
**log time** = the timer + the slept ms accounted so far, so the period
cadence, the newest entry's end and the reply's age all hold across sleeps and
the host's dating (newest − k × period) stays right. The sleep is laid onto
the periods it spans: none crossed, and the frozen and live periods merge; else
the first to close keeps its frozen contents plus its share of the sleep, each
later one holds only the sleep's share (no `5C`, a dark load), and the period
open now keeps the intake since the wake. Each entry's level is the level
before the sleep less the sleep's energy up to that entry's end, as the count
debits it, stopping where the debit stopped (the floor, the cut): a night
that reached 1% early shows it early, not a line to 1% across the night. No period closes between the wake and the accounting,
and until then `HC_BATTLOG`'s version byte is **0xFF** (`BATTERY_LOG_PENDING`):
its age would date the old entries hours too recently, so
`ak820battery.py log` retries.

**The RGB model is per colour** (riding along; the curve:
`history/power-bench-2026-10-15/rgb-curve-20261019/`). The driver keeps three
channel sums of the committed frames instead of one, and the model weighs
them: **8.64 mW while any LED is lit, plus 3,681 / 2,565 / 2,322 nW per unit
of the red / green / blue sum over the 81 LEDs**, replacing Phase 2's single
2.857 µW per unit. (Until 10-21 the slopes were 3,636 / 2,533 / 2,294: the
fit counted 82 LEDs, so the RGB read ~1.2% low; `plans/BACKLOG.md`, "The RGB
model's 82 LEDs".) Against the map every step's paired mean is within 0.6%
(a single hold within 0.92%), purple within 0.2%. The log's
single `led_pm` cannot recover the split, but its energy field carries the
model's total.

**Measured on the host** (`scripts/battery_sim`, **148 cases**, sanitizers
included): 17 sleep scenarios (`sleeps.c`): the energy, time left as hours of
use, the floor and the cut, an unknown level, a stale band hold, remainders,
the relaxation, the log within / across / far beyond a period, pending,
the boundary between the wake and the accounting, USB, a v7 replay with
sleeps, a sleep reaching the floor early, and a replayed entry partly asleep.
The log checks are exact: every `5C` report in exactly one entry, the energy
to its carry, the open period's share of the sleep. Ten planted bugs, each
caught (among them the old line-to-the-final-level, a dropped carry, live
reports in the frozen period, a mixed entry played as wholly awake). With the per-colour model the two
drains' replays (R3 and 2e) are worst **+1.9 / +2.1 points** (were +1.1 /
+1.2 on the single-channel model): the logs record only the total drive,
which the replay splits evenly over the three colours. Still inside 3. **Not yet measured: the
sleep's energy against the logger across a drain, high, mid and low (R5).**

## Hardware facts

**The pack** (JD's unit, printed on the cell): `JKJ606090 4000mAh 72.9g,
3.7V 14.8Wh`, date code `202512.30`, Shenzhen Jiekejie. Li/Ni/Co/Mn with a
graphite anode: **NMC**. The other unit (the white one) had no pack fitted as of
2026-09-09.

**Its protection board is unidentified.** No spec sheet for `JKJ606090` or its
maker turned up (searched 2026-10-01). Other makers' 606090 4000 mAh packs
specify an over-discharge cut-off of **2.75 V** (some 3.0 V), e.g.
[lipobattery.us's LP606090](https://www.lipobattery.us/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/LP606090-4000mAh.pdf),
which would put this pack's trip **below** `5C`'s zero at ~3.161 V — but that
is the genre, not this part. The chip markings on the protection board (under
the tape at the lead end) would settle it.

**The slider is labelled POWER on the PCB.** It selects the power source as
well as the mode ([hardware.md](hardware.md)): BT → cable reboots the MCU.

**No analogue pin can see the pack.** AIN0..AIN15 are P2.0..P2.15, all matrix
columns, RGB rows or the Win Lock LED. The op-amp and comparator inputs are RGB
rows (B4, B5, A11), the flash's WP (B2), the encoder (A10) and the BOOT pin
(B3). There is nowhere to wire a divider, even by hand.

**VDD by power state** (the SN32 measuring itself against its internal 2.0 V
reference, ±2% per part):

| state | VDD | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| cable position, USB in | 4.79 V (4.53 V charging) | USB, through a low-drop path |
| BT position, USB in | 4.47 V idle; 4.12-4.31 V charging (~4.19 V through constant current from flat, rising through the taper) | USB through a diode |
| BT position, unplugged | **3.90 V, flat** | the buck-boost's output, fed from the pack |

**It is a buck-boost.** With the pack at 3.84 V, VDD read 3.901 V — above its
own input, which no linear regulator can do. Over the 09-25 run the pack fell
4.18 → 3.74 V while VDD moved +3 mV; VDD first sagged visibly (~3.87 V) with the
pack at 3.30 V. So VDD says which supply is on, never how full the pack is.

**VDD tracks charge current, qualitatively.** On USB in the BT position the
diode's drop scales with current: flat at ~4193 mV through constant current on
09-28 (03:00-07:00), then 4241 → 4268 → 4300 as the taper set in. ⚠️ Do not
threshold it: in the cable position VDD reads 4530 mV *while charging*, and USB
voltage, cable and temperature all move it. "Full" comes from CHRG.

**The draw at full white.** The 09-25 run lasted ~52-63 h (pastel for its
first ~4.7 h; its end fell in a 5.7 h unobserved window, and a 20 min charge
mid-run added an unknown few hours). From the label's 14.8 Wh that is
~0.23-0.28 W at the pack, ~60-75 mA at 3.7 V — if the label is honest. Lights-off draw is unmeasured. The LEDs are
multiplexed 1/18, so even full white draws modestly. **An hour unplugged at
full pastel RGB was put back in 23 minutes** of charging (09-24, 19:53 → 20:16;
this charger gives at most 1 A).

## The charger (ASC4056)

A TP4056-class linear charger, ESOP8 (`fpb/ajazz-ak820-pro/docs/ASC4056.pdf`,
in Chinese). **Pin 5 is BAT** (the pack's +), a much easier probe point than
the pack connector. CHRG (pin 7) and STDBY/DONE (pin 6) are open-drain, read on
B16 and B17 with pull-ups. The datasheet's table:

| state | CHRG | DONE |
|---|---|---|
| charging | low | high |
| full | high | low |
| no battery | pulsing ~2 Hz | low |
| fault (temperature, VIN < 3.8 V, VIN < VBAT) | high | high |

What the board actually shows:

- **CHRG works, slowly.** It released ~9½ h into a charge from a deeply
  discharged pack (09-28, pack 4.18 V), with `5C` at 100 for the last ~5½ h;
  a top-up of a full pack released after ~11 min. It answers "has charging
  stopped", not "how full". **The gauge's "full" is CHRG released with `5C` at
  100.**
- **DONE has never been seen low on USB**, not even after termination (09-24:
  CHRG went high with USB in and B17 stayed high, which the table calls a
  fault). B17 is probably not DONE, or DONE is not connected.
- ⚠️ **On battery DONE reads low**, in every entry of the 09-28 drain log: with
  no VIN the pin evidently loses its pull-up. So on battery it says nothing.
- The charging LED: the stock firmware lit it in the cable position, so the
  pack charges there too (JD, 2026-09-25). Ours leaves it off (far too bright);
  the bolt on the battery row reads the same pin. ⚠️ **Both are firmware-driven**
  (the LED is B18, `CHARGING_LED_BRIGHTNESS` 0). **In the bootloader nothing
  shows whether the pack is charging**, so "when the charge finished" can only
  come from a running board's CHRG.
- **Charges from flat, timed against the sensor and VDD**: these are timings,
  not measured CC/CV phases (Phase 1b's B).

  | charge | `5C` reaches the clamp | VDD starts rising | CHRG released |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 09-28 | 3 h 55 min | ~4 h | ~9.5 h |
  | 10-01 | 4.0-4.2 h | ~4.25 h | ~9.4 h |
  | 10-09, from the RGB cut (0%), not flat | ~3 h 40-50 min (07:24-07:34) | ~4.0 h | ~9 h 05-10 min |

  During the stretch before the clamp, the board's own load moves VDD: LED
  drive 1000 → 499 ‰ raised it **+59 mV** on 10-01. Records:
  `history/battery-2026-10-01-charge/`, the 09-28 rows in
  `history/battery-2026-09-25/readings.csv`, and for 10-09
  `history/battery-2026-10-08-partial-charge/readings.csv` (gate 8).
- **The charging display reads low on the fitted curve.** It is
  `curve(V − 150 mV)`, which gave **18.4% 1.7 h into the 10-04 charge from
  flat**, where the curve predicted ~18.6%. By charge time it would be roughly
  30%; that figure is unmeasured. Then it races to ~90% once `5C` clamps.
  Phase 1b's B replaces it with a time model bounded by the voltage.
  **Superseded:** the model has been on the board since flash 2 (10-08), and
  gate 8's charge from 0 passed on it ("The gauge as built, Phase 1b").

## The pack connector, and tapping it (2026-09-26; corrected 2026-10-14; confirmed 2026-10-15)

**3-pin, 1.0 mm pitch, JST SH or compatible.** **Confirmed 10-15:** a kit's
SH plug mates with the board's header, and the pack's plug with the kit's SH
header (Ulifebet "Mini Micro JST SH 1.0mm Pitch 3-Pin", 20 sets of wired
plugs and loose board headers, Amazon ASIN `B0F32RWDC6`). ⚠️ **This section
said 1.25 mm (Molex PicoBlade) until 10-14, and that was wrong.** A 1.25 mm
extension built into the INA228 harness did not mate with the pack's plug.
On calipers the pack's plug read **~0.94 mm** pin to pin, and the extension
1.25 mm (JD, 10-14). The 09-26 reading of 5.45 mm across the housing was
wrong, and it is what the 1.25 mm order came from.

| family | pitch | 3-pin housing |
|---|---|---|
| **JST SH** | **1.0 mm** | ~4.6 mm |
| PicoBlade / JST GH | 1.25 mm | ~5.5 mm |

⚠️ Other 1.0 mm families (JST SR, Molex Pico-Lock) look alike and do not
mate with each other; compare the latch with the pack's plug before buying. JST
part numbers, if it is SH: plug housing `SHR-03V-S`, side-entry header
`SM03B-SRSS-TB`. ⚠️ **SH is wire-to-board: there is no ready-made 3-pin
male-to-female SH extension.** Kits sold as "male and female" pair wired plugs
(the same part as the pack's plug, which goes into the keyboard) with loose
board headers; the in-line socket the pack's plug goes into is one of those
headers with three wires soldered to its legs. The contacts are too small to
crimp by hand for no good reason; buy them pre-crimped.

**The pinout, from JD's unit:** red **+**, black **−**, white **NTC**. The
silkscreen beside the header says `NTC`. ⚠️ The black wire is nearly invisible
against the black PCB in photographs — count conductors at the pack, not on
screen.

⚠️ **The NTC is present and the firmware cannot read it.** It goes to the
charger's temperature-fault input, and every AIN pin is already spoken for
(above). **Pack temperature is reachable only by an external tap** — worth
having, because the curve we are fitting shifts with temperature and a
multi-day run on a desk sees several degrees.

### The tap

A **3-pin extension** in line with the pack (a wired SH plug at the keyboard
end, a hand-wired SH header at the pack end, above), with the conductors
brought out to a logger. Nothing is soldered to the pack, and no pack terminal is
ever exposed — which is the safety argument as much as the convenience one: the
09-25 run had **three accidental probe shorts**, one of which rebooted the board.
A permanent tap removes the poking that caused them.

- **Voltage only, in parallel.** ADS1115 (16-bit, ±4.096 V, ~0.1 mV) with a 2:1
  divider, or a logging DMM and no code at all.
- A 100k/100k divider draws 21 µA: ~2.5 mAh over five days out of 4000. Ignorable.
- The pack moves ~3-27 mV/h through most of a run at full white (slowest on
  the ~4.0 V plateau), and ~65 mV/h at the end (09-25: 3.68 → 3.30 V in 5.8 h). A sample every 10-60 s is plenty.
- Take the NTC out to the same logger for temperature.
- ⚠️ **A current shunt in the pack path drops its I×R between the pack and
  everything downstream, the CH582F included**, so `5C` would read lower than
  the pack by that much (~6-8 mV at a 0.1 Ω shunt and ~70 mA: about a count).
  Measure voltage on the pack side of any shunt, or use a 0.01 Ω one.

**Before connecting anything**, two checks at the header with the pack in:
`+` to `−` reads pack voltage (~4.0 V), and NTC to `−` reads ~10 kΩ at room
temperature for a 10k thermistor. That identifies the pins properly instead of
trusting wire colour. A meter reading that wanders is probe contact, not
lighting: full versus off moves the pack only 10 mV.

## A real fuel gauge, retrofitted (a plan, not done)

⚠️ **Optional and personal.** This modifies one unit and helps nobody else
running this firmware. The filtered-`5C` gauge needs no soldering and no
purchase, and holds ±5% everywhere but the curve's flat top (70-85%, up to 8.3
points low on 10-10); the retrofit is **a refinement, not an
enabler**: ±1-2%, no curve to fit or refit, and ageing compensation our fitted
curve will not have. Its load and relaxation modelling is largely wasted here,
given the 10 mV sag. It is a want, not a need.

**The part:** a **MAX17048**. ModelGauge, so **no sense resistor** — it sits
across the pack and reports over I²C, ~$5 on a breakout. ⚠️ Still
voltage-derived, so it is not coulomb counting; what it does better than us is
the voltage-to-level model, not the voltage. (JD's 2026-09-29 DigiKey cart,
[`plans/parts/`](../plans/parts/digikey-order-2026-09-29.csv), not yet ordered,
has an INA228 as a **current logger** for the power-mode work; a MAX17055 or
MAX1726x would be the permanent coulomb-counting option.)

### ⭐ The board already has an I²C bus

**This is what makes the mod reasonable rather than fiddly.** `halconf.h:15-21`:

> External PCF8563 RTC on **P0.14/P0.15** via the ChibiOS software (bit-banged)
> I2C fallback LLD … The SN32 HW I2C peripheral cannot reach those pins.

So SDA and SCL are already routed, already working, and already driven by a
proven driver. Consequences:

- **No free GPIO needed.** The pin audit below is still useful reference, but
  the retrofit does not consume any of it.
- **No new driver needed.** `SW_I2C_USE_I2C1` gives `I2CD1`; add a second
  device to the same bus.
- **No address conflict.** MAX17048 is `0x36`, PCF8563 is `0x51`.
- **No 0.5 mm soldering.** The targets are the PCF8563's own pins (SO8/TSSOP8,
  1.27 or 0.65 mm pitch) or its surrounding passives — an entirely different
  difficulty class from a QFP leg.

### Wiring

| MAX17048 | goes to | note |
|---|---|---|
| `VDD` | **pack +** | it measures its own supply; this is the whole point |
| `GND` | **pack −** | common with board ground |
| `SDA` | P0.14 or P0.15 | whichever `rtc.c` assigns; take it at the PCF8563 |
| `SCL` | the other | |
| `CTG`/`ALRT` | leave | not needed for polling |

### ⚠️ Three electrical checks before committing

1. **Logic levels across two supplies.** The MAX17048's `VDD` is the **pack**
   (up to 4.2 V) while the SN32 runs at 3.3 V. Its I²C thresholds are specified
   against its own `VDD`, so confirm `VIH` is met by a 3.3 V bus at a 4.2 V
   `VDD` (0.7 × 4.2 = 2.94 V, so it should pass, but **check the datasheet
   rather than this arithmetic**). Nothing exceeds 3.3 V on the SN32 side, so
   that direction is safe.
2. **Pull-ups may not exist as components.** ⚠️ `SW_I2C_USE_OPENDRAIN FALSE`
   means the driver emulates open-drain by **switching the pin to input to
   release the line** — so something else pulls it high, possibly the SN32's
   internal pull-ups rather than external resistors. **Look for them on the
   board before planning to solder to them.**
3. **Bus capacitance.** Adding a second device and a length of wire slows the
   rise time, and internal pull-ups are weak (tens of kΩ). If the bus is
   relying on them, **fit a proper 4.7 kΩ external pull-up** on each line as
   part of the mod. You are soldering anyway.

### ⚠️ The bus is rationed, and for a reason

`ak820pro.c:585`: *"RTC I2C (port A) glitches the flash SPI1 pins (A12/A13)
mid-DMA."* I²C transactions are deliberately limited to **one per main-loop
pass, and only with the LCD DMA idle** (`docs/clock.md`). A MAX17048 read every
30 s is negligible traffic, but it must go **through that gate, not around it**
— schedule it the way `rtc_task()` is scheduled, and do not add a transaction
anywhere that can run mid-blit.

### Solder targets, easiest first

1. **External I²C pull-up resistors**, if they exist — 0402/0603 pads, trivial.
2. **The PCF8563's SDA/SCL pins** — SO8 or TSSOP8, very manageable.
3. **A via on either net** — scrape the mask, tin, done.
4. The MCU legs, pins 46/47 — last resort, and unnecessary given the above.

Pack `+`/`−` come from the connector tap (see the connector section), so no
soldering to the pack itself.

### The pin audit (reference, no longer load-bearing)

Kept because it is useful for any firmware work on this board, not because the
gauge needs it. QMK's names map as **`A`→P0, `B`→P1, `C`→P2, `D`→P3**, verified
because `keyboard.json`'s rows land on physical pins **38-43**, exactly matching
`fpb/ajazz-ak820-pro`'s "row pins 38-43", and the columns on 15-30. The MCU is
an **HFD80CP100** — an SN32F299F clone, **80-pin LQFP, 0.5 mm pitch**, marked
`U3`, pin-1 dimple at the bottom-left of the package with the marking readable.
Datasheet: `docs/SN32F299_V1.8_EN.pdf` in that repo.

| pins | use |
|---|---|
| 1-6, 8-14, 73, 75-78 | RGB matrix rows (`SN32F2XX_RGB_MATRIX_ROW_PINS`) |
| 15-27, 29, 30 | key matrix columns (shared with the RGB columns) |
| 38-43 | key matrix rows |
| 28, 65, 68 | WinLock LED (`C15`), indicator (`D15`), charging LED (`B18`) |
| 36, 37 | dip switch (`B12`, `B13`) |
| 44, 45, 64 | panel `RST` (`A17`), `BKL` (`A16`), `DC` (`D14`) |
| 46, 47 | **PCF8563 I²C (P0.14/P0.15)** |
| 50, 52, 59 | SPI `SCK` (`D0`), `MOSI` (`D2`), `SS` (`B8`) |
| 72, 74 | encoder (`A10`), `BOOT` (`B3`) |
| 48, 49, 66, 67, 69, 70, 71 | flash/BT/misc (`A13`, `A12`, `B16`, `B17`, `B0`, `B1`, `B2`) |

**Free — no firmware reference anywhere:** `A0`/`A1`/`A2`/`A3` (pins **31-34**,

⚠️ **`D1` (pin 51) looks free and is not.** `SPI_MISO_PIN` is `NO_PIN`, so it
has no firmware references — but it is SPI0's hardware MISO, sitting between
`SCK` (50) and `MOSI` (52), and the flash almost certainly reads through it.

⚠️ **"Unused by firmware" is not "unconnected."** Probe before trusting.

⚠️ **The RGB rows include `D10`-`D13`, which are `LXIN`/`LXOUT`/`XIN`/`XOUT`.**
Both crystal pairs are repurposed as LED drive, so **the board has no crystal at
all** and runs on the internal RC — which is why `config.h` warns the oscillator
is per-unit and temperature-dependent, and why the clock needs a host to
discipline it.

## The shutdown of 2026-09-24 (unexplained)

After a few hours unplugged at full brightness the board went completely dark
and came back **only when the cable was plugged in**. The pack was **nowhere
near flat**: it had charged for only ~45 minutes before the charger stopped, and
read full the same evening. Our firmware has **no sleep, timeout or power-down
path at all** (the idle ladder is compiled out). So something cut the
battery-side power, and USB (which reaches VDD by its own path) bypassed it.
Suspects:

1. an idle or disconnect power-off in the CH582F or the power circuit (the
   stock firmware has a "deep sleep" state, `g_connection_mode` 0x0D, that
   ours never takes part in);
2. the pack's protection circuit tripping (a charger connection resets it).

Not reproduced. If it happens again: note the time and how long the board had
been idle, then try a key press, then the slider flip with the cable still out,
then the cable.

⭐ **A known cause gives the same symptom (2026-09-30, ~20:44).** A probe slip
shorted the pack connector mid-run, cable out: the board went **dark and stayed
dead**. A slider flip to the cable position and back, **still with no cable**,
brought it back (a fresh boot; the RAM log was gone). So a dark board on battery
is recoverable without a charger. That fits the pack protector's short-circuit
trip, which in DW01-class protectors releases once the load is removed — the
cable position disconnects the pack — but **the protector in this pack is
unidentified, so that is a hypothesis**, and a flip would also reset any latch
in the board's own power path. It does make suspect 2 concrete: something that
trips the protector, then holds it tripped while the board stays connected.

## The idle ladder and the power modes (Phase 2, `power.c`)

`Fn`+`B` (`PWR_MODE`) cycles **Always on**, **Balanced** (the default),
**Power saver** and **Lights out**, shown as an overlay and persisted (kb
datablock byte 6; a flash resets it to Balanced and says so once). The enum
keeps the first names, `POWER_MODE_FULL`, `NORMAL`, `SAVER` and `MINIMAL`
(Full power, Normal power, Power saver and Minimal mode until `26fcafaeca`).
On battery the bolt's slot shows the mode as a bulb ([display.md](display.md),
"Battery row"). On battery only, with the timings and Power saver's ceiling
of [`plans/POWER-MODES-REVISION-PLAN.md`](../plans/POWER-MODES-REVISION-PLAN.md):

| | Always on | Balanced | Power saver | Lights out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RGB while typing | as set | as set | value capped at 128 | off |
| RGB dimmed (its value capped at 64 of 255) | never | 10 min idle | 1 min | -- |
| RGB and backlight off, deep sleep | never | 20 min | 3 min | 1 min |

The screen goes off when the board sleeps (its dim needs the 20 kHz tick the
sleep stops), and the RGB with it: no mode passes through the RGB-off,
screen-at-level-4 stage (`POWER_STAGE_LIGHTS_OFF`) any more. It stays in the
enum, because the stage numbers are on the wire, and the bench can still hold
it (op 14 sub 2).
- **Before the revision** (`26fcafaeca` to `8b06e4dcc4`): Balanced dimmed at
  1 min, went RGB-off at 5 and slept at 7; Power saver at 30 s, 2 and 3; Lights
  out slept after 30 s; and Power saver typed at full brightness.
- **Below 15%** every mode but Lights out takes Power saver's lighting, its
  128 while typing included, and Balanced sleeps at Power saver's 3 min.
  Always on never sleeps, at low battery too.
- **Deep sleep** and what holds it off: [hardware.md](hardware.md), "Deep
  sleep"; the gauge across it: "The gauge across a deep sleep", above.

**Power saver's ceiling** is applied as the dim's is, in
`rgb_matrix_hsv_to_rgb`, while typing: a value over 128 becomes 128. Under
QMK's CIE curve that is a drive of 48 of 255, about half as bright to the eye
and a quarter of the LEDs' power. A board already at 128 or below is not
changed, which is why it is a ceiling and not half the user's value: halving
a dim board again can move its colours (a dim magenta toward red). DRIFT,
whose brightness scales its colours after the conversion, caps its own
value knob instead, so it saves about half its LED power, not three
quarters.

**Lights out** keeps the RGB and the backlight off even while typing (~107 mW,
~6 days a charge by the model), and lights the backlight at the user's level
for 4 s after a message appears in the status band (a setting's overlay, an
alert, a new link state with words, the pair gesture) and while `Fn`+`D`'s
pages are open, so a key that shows state never seems dead.

Any key or knob turn restores the mode's typing state within a tenth of a
second, and the key types as normal: Always on's and Balanced's full
lighting, Power saver's 128. Every stage is a cap, never a change to the
user's settings. The dim caps the lighting's own value
(`rgb_matrix_hsv_to_rgb`), so a colour keeps its hue. Whenever what is
applied changes, the effect's init pass re-runs (`rgb_matrix_reinit_effect`,
a local hook in `quantum/rgb_matrix/`), so effects that redraw only some LEDs
a frame go dark at once and come back whole:
- at a stage change;
- when the typing ceiling moves with no stage change: `Fn`+`B` into or out of
  Power saver, the cable in or out, the low cap's start and end.

Below 15% the lighting follows Power saver whatever the mode, and says so
once a discharge ("Saving power", on a lit screen; a real charge re-arms it);
the bulb shows Power saver's then, except in Lights out
(`power_lights_mode()`).

Measured (R1, A8): the RGB is 189 of the board's 306 mW at JD's settings,
and JD's keyboard sits idle ~88% of the time. With deep sleep, the model puts
Balanced at ~12½ days a charge at JD's use against Always on's ~2.7, and
Power saver at ~21 ([power.md](power.md), "The power modes at JD's use"; R5
measures it). Deep sleep is built (above); light sleep and the radio's own
idle are not.

## Open questions

- **How `5C` frames arrive**: replies, a stream, or both (above).
- **Whether the line holds on another unit**, and across temperature.
- ~~**`CHARGE_IR_MV`**: measure it as the step in `5C` at the moment of
  unplugging mid-charge.~~ Moot since flash 2: B3 measured the post-unplug
  relaxation and the charge rate instead, and the model dropped the I×R guess.
- **Where the charge's knee really is.** `L_KNEE` is an extrapolation, and
  `K_CC` rises with the starting level. A current logger on the bench (the
  INA228 kit, ordered) can map both.
- **The flat top's 8.3 points.** Whether a different estimator, temperature
  or another curve shape there can bring 70-85% inside ±5%.
- **The LCD's bright flash during a write** (seen once, 10-01): the plan's
  C4 hypothesis, a page erase freezing the backlight PWM, is untested.
- **Flash endurance in years** (the plan's C3; above).
- **The 85-at-~4.00 V reading** of 09-26 (above).
- **~10-15 unexplained flash writes** in 22 h on the old build (blinks at
  09-28 15:50 and 09-29 12:33 matched no known write). Phase 1b's counters
  named the steady writer on later builds (the clock's period); these two
  were never matched.
- **The 09-24 shutdown** (above).
